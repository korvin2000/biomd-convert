A small image on a 1998-2010 page is one of three things, and the migration treats
each completely differently. A documented table of known shared assets has already
been consulted and does not contain this one, which is why you are being asked.

Answer:

- **PICTURE** — content. A photograph, a scan, a cover, a portrait, a score
  fragment, a thumbnail linking to a larger scan. It keeps its image directive.
- **ICON** — a control or a mark drawn as a picture: a pager arrow, a link marker, an
  audio or score badge, an e-mail glyph, a bullet the author drew. It becomes a text
  glyph, chosen from the closed vocabulary below.
- **DECORATION** — a spacer, a rule, a divider strip, a background tile, a hit
  counter. It is dropped entirely.
- **UNCERTAIN** — cannot tell. The image is kept as a picture, which is the safe
  default, and a review item is filed.

Evidence, in order of weight:

1. **Geometry.** Below roughly 24 px in both dimensions nothing can be depicted, so
   the image is ICON or DECORATION. A strip much wider than it is tall, with no label,
   is DECORATION.
2. **Containment.** Inside a link, an icon-sized image is a control. Standing alone in
   a cell beside a caption, it is a picture however small.
3. **What the page says about it.** A label, a caption or a neighbouring sentence that
   names what is depicted makes it a PICTURE. A file name never counts as evidence;
   ignore it entirely.
4. **Recurrence across the page.** A mark repeated at the head of every row of a list
   is an ICON. A picture appears once.

**The false friend is a linked thumbnail** — a small content image whose link points at
a larger scan. Size does not separate it from an icon; a caption or a naming neighbour
does. When a caption is present, answer PICTURE.
